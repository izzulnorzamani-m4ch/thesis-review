# Level expectations

Calibrate every judgement to the level. The question is always "is this good enough for *this* level?", not "is this perfect?".

## Undergraduate / Final Year Project (FYP)

**Core expectation:** the student can carry out a bounded, well-organised piece of research or development competently, with guidance.

- **Originality:** not required. Applying known methods to a local context, replicating a study, or building and evaluating a system is fine. Don't penalise a modest contribution.
- **Literature review:** shows awareness of the main relevant work and some ability to compare sources. Some summarising is acceptable; the review should still lead logically to the project's aim.
- **Method:** appropriate and clearly described; justification can be lighter than at postgraduate level, but the choice shouldn't be arbitrary.
- **Analysis:** correct and clearly presented. Basic statistics or thematic analysis done properly beats ambitious analysis done badly.
- **Discussion:** relates findings to the objectives and at least some literature; acknowledges obvious limitations.
- **Typical length:** roughly 8,000–20,000 words depending on discipline and institution; development projects may be shorter in prose with system documentation.
- **Common FYP problems:** objectives too broad for the timeframe; "literature review" that is a list of definitions; results with no discussion; system projects with no meaningful evaluation; conclusion that just repeats the abstract.

## Master's

Distinguish **research master's** (thesis is the whole degree) from **coursework/mixed-mode** (a dissertation or project component). Expectations are similar in kind; research master's work is larger and deeper.

**Core expectation:** independent, competent research showing critical engagement with the field and sound, justified methodology.

- **Originality:** a contribution is expected but can be modest — a new context, population, combination of methods, or a careful extension of existing work.
- **Literature review:** clearly critical and synthesised (organised by theme or debate, not by author); identifies a specific, credible gap.
- **Theory/framework:** a conceptual or theoretical framework is usually expected and should actually shape the method and analysis, not just appear in Chapter 2.
- **Method:** fully justified, including alternatives considered, sampling, instrument validity/reliability or qualitative trustworthiness, and ethics.
- **Discussion:** interprets results against the literature, explains unexpected findings, discusses implications.
- **Typical length:** coursework dissertation ~15,000–25,000 words; research master's ~30,000–60,000 words.
- **Common master's problems:** gap asserted rather than demonstrated; framework decorative; instrument validity unaddressed; discussion that restates results; overclaiming generalisability from small samples.

## PhD

**Core expectation:** an original, significant contribution to knowledge, produced through independent research of publishable quality, by someone who can now be trusted to do research unsupervised.

- **Originality and contribution:** must be explicit and defensible. The student should be able to state in a few sentences what is now known (or possible) that wasn't before. If you can't find a clear contribution statement, that's a Critical issue.
- **Literature review:** comprehensive and authoritative; positions the work within debates, not just topics; shows mastery, including recent work (check that the review is current to within a couple of years of submission).
- **Theory:** engages with theory at a level where the thesis could inform or challenge it, not just apply it.
- **Method:** rigorous, defensible against an expert examiner, with limitations and threats to validity analysed rather than listed.
- **Coherence:** the thesis reads as one sustained argument. In thesis-by-publication formats, linking chapters must make the papers cohere.
- **Publishability:** substantial parts should be of a standard publishable in peer-reviewed venues.
- **Typical length:** ~60,000–100,000 words (varies widely; STEM often shorter, humanities longer).
- **Common PhD problems:** contribution diffuse or overstated; chapters that read as separate studies with no through-line; review that stops several years before submission; method chapter that would not survive an expert's questions; implications for theory missing.

## When the level is unclear

Infer from title page, declaration, length, depth and the student's description. State the level you assumed at the top of the report, and if a chapter would be judged very differently at the adjacent level, say so briefly (e.g. "this would be strong for a coursework master's but thin for a research master's").
