# Summary rubric

The rubric gives the student a one-glance profile of the thesis. It sits at the top of the report; the chapter-by-chapter feedback underneath is the evidence for each score. Every score must be traceable to specific issues or strengths in the detailed feedback — a score with nothing behind it in the report is just a guess.

**If the student shares their university's own rubric or examiner form, use that instead** (its criteria, scale and weightings), and map your findings onto it. The rubric below is the generic default.

## Scale

Score each criterion **relative to the level being assessed** (see `level-expectations.md`). A 4 for an FYP and a 4 for a PhD mean different things.

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Excellent | Would satisfy a demanding examiner at this level; at most minor polish |
| 4 | Good | Solid; some Major-but-fixable issues or several Minor ones |
| 3 | Adequate | Meets the basic expectation for the level but with clear Major weaknesses |
| 2 | Weak | Falls short of the level; substantial revision needed |
| 1 | Inadequate | Missing or fundamentally flawed; a Critical issue sits here |
| n/a | Not assessed | Part not provided or not applicable (e.g. no separate framework in a humanities thesis) |

A criterion containing a **Critical** issue scores no higher than 2.

## Criteria

1. **Problem, gap and research questions** — Is the problem evidenced, the gap specific and credible, and are the RQs/objectives clear, consistent and answerable?
2. **Engagement with literature** — Synthesis rather than summary; critical evaluation; current and relevant; leads to the gap.
3. **Theoretical / conceptual framework** — Appropriate, justified, and actually used in method and interpretation.
4. **Methodology and rigour** — Design matches the RQs; sampling/case selection, data collection and analysis justified; validity/trustworthiness and ethics addressed.
5. **Analysis and use of evidence** — Findings are accurate, sufficient and well presented; claims are supported; facts are correct.
6. **Argument and coherence (golden thread)** — RQs carried through method, results and conclusions; chapters build one argument; claims proportionate to evidence.
7. **Contribution and originality** — Clear, defensible statement of what is new, at the level expected (modest for FYP, substantial for PhD).
8. **Conclusions, implications and limitations** — Each RQ answered; implications follow from findings; limitations substantive; future research sensible.
9. **Referencing and academic integrity** — Citations and reference list consistent and complete; one style applied; sources appropriate; quotations properly attributed.
10. **Writing and presentation** — Clarity, academic register, structure and signposting, figures/tables, formatting.

## Presenting it

```
| Criterion | Score (1–5) | Basis (one line, pointing to the detailed section) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem, gap and RQs | 3 | Clear RQ; gap overstated (see Introduction, Abstract) |
...
```

Follow the table with two lines: **strongest areas** and **weakest areas**.

Don't add up the scores into a total or percentage unless the student's own university rubric works that way — theses aren't examined by summing criteria, and a single number hides the profile that tells the student where to work. The overall readiness verdict comes from the most serious issues, not from an average.
